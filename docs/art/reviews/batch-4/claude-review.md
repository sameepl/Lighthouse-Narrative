**What works**
- **Palette:** The set reads as one family. Blue is the structure, amber is the human light, and cyan is limited to sensors. The title ship's cyan nose and the pawn's sensor arcs and corner brackets both follow that rule.
- **Text, faces, characters:** None appear in the art.
- **Hall:** The glass office, two blank amber job sheets, ribs and recorder dot are all visible. The dot is small but legible at 1200 wide.
- **Pawn:** The cockpit, viewport and sensor arc read clearly. The amber dot gives a single focal point. At the 1200 width it is clear that the turret is a folded shape.
- **Endings:** The two share an identical corridor. The open ending has a warm door and light spill. The closed ending has a cool door with one amber lamp. The contrast carries the meaning with no extra decoration, so the pair is the strongest in the set.
- **Header then body:** The art sits above the text, and the fade into the body background is smooth in both themes. Body text is legible in every capture.

**Concrete issues (non-blocking, worth a quick fix)**
1. **Light theme at 40% art:** The art turns into a gray haze. The hall, title and pawn lose most of their structure. Amber survives, but the blue structure turns to mush. This is a legibility drop rather than a failure. Focal elements are still findable, but the title ring and the hall ribs are faint. Consider a light-theme art opacity of about 50–55%, or a slightly darker line weight.
2. **Title at 390 wide:** The station and ship are small, roughly 170px across, and sit in the top-left of a mostly empty header. In the light theme the ship almost merges with the ring. It is readable but weak.
3. **Hard crop at the fade edge:** At 1200 wide the art is a 560px column with a visible edge against the page background. In dark mode this is a subtle rectangle edge, and in light mode it is mildly noticeable. A slightly wider fade, or a full-bleed header, would hide it.

**Owner taste, not blockers**
- The large empty body area between art and text. That is probably screenshot layout, not the real UI.
- The dark-mode art is very low contrast toward its lower edge. The fade is intentional and fits "quiet".
- Whether the open door's amber is too saturated and flat compared with the restrained amber elsewhere. It works as the emotional peak, but the owner may want it softened a little.
- Hall composition: the central office is a bit small relative to the corridor. The hall reads clearly at 1200 but is more cramped at 390.

**Recommendation:** Send it to the owner. Flag the light-theme 40% haze and the small title art at 390 as the two things to watch. If the owner wants a change, the likeliest one is raising the light-theme opacity.
