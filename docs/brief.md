# Game Brief

Source: written by the owner's campaign-planning thread, pasted in full. This is source material and a theme guide, not a specification to copy beat for beat. See `docs/game-design.md` for what the game actually does.

## 1. Setting snapshot

Tone: space opera with dark shades. Exploration and mystery come first. Choices matter and doing right costs something. Tech is near-future-plus: jump drives, fusion power, crewed small ships, laser and missile weapons, no magic, no psychics on screen. Credits are tight and every hull needs upkeep.

The universe feels like a frontier stitched together by paperwork. There is no empire to call for help. Local authorities, yard cooperatives and independent crews hold the lanes together with charters, berth rights and arms registers. Pirates and hijackers prey on the traffic. The player's people have just won the right to be taken seriously. Past that, a long-buried mystery is surfacing: a sealed folder, a cut continuity mast, a number that matches no chart.

## 2. Protagonist

Marcus Torin, a former merchant-navy officer turned captain of a small independent alliance. He is the player and is never written for: the game never decides his speech, thoughts or actions. He holds a main ship, the Errant Pawn (fast, armoured, quiet, a good escort), and leads a fleet of four hulls with about 40 named crew and allies. His home base is Hearthline, a neutral ring-station run by a council called the Compact. He starts at a quiet moment after a six-month stretch of yard work and sales, with cash in the bank, a shaky alliance with the station's leaders, and an unexplained memory fragment he does not understand.

## 3. Sample scene (most important)

**Scene: "The Written Clause"** (about 5 minutes, one conversation, three approaches)

**Location:** The Compact's hall at Hearthline, a low-ceilinged room above the berths. The viewport shows your four ships docked at their arms.

**NPCs**

- **Renata** (Compact chair): short declarative sentences, states the limit first, counts things aloud, never gives a loose yes. Tired, honest, and holding a frontier together with law.
- **Priya** (Seneschal, Marcus's liaison): warm, quick, reads fine print for a living, quietly on your side.
- **Halvorsen** (Compact officer): says little; watches for any sign you'll cause the Compact trouble.

**Prose (the voice we want):**

> Renata lays the charter on the table between you and does not push it across.
>
> "Three pages," she says. "Berth, standing, arms. Hull by hull, with hours." She taps the third page. "This is the one you want. And the one that costs."
>
> Priya reads upside down, lips moving. She sets two fingers on a clause and says nothing.
>
> "Your people carry peace-bonded weapons on the ring. Fine. Your ships fire only in defence of life, ship and traffic. Also fine. But I will not write that down for you, because the moment it is written, I must write it for everyone." Renata counts on her fingers. "The merchants. The tugs. The visiting navy. Every hull. And they have asked, Captain. Many times."
>
> Halvorsen looks at the viewport, at your ships, then at you.

**The problem:** Your crews already defend Hearthline's traffic, yet you hold only an informal understanding. A hijack on the lanes could land your people in an after-action review with no protection on paper. Renata can give you a written self-defence clause, but it binds the Compact to give the same to everyone.

**Approaches**

1. **Press for parity** (Persuade or Diplomat). The clause is written for all hulls.
   - _Success:_ Your people are covered, and so is everyone else. Merchants trust you more, but Renata owes you nothing further and the Compact grows warier of your fleet's size.
   - _Failure:_ Renata holds the line. You leave with the berth and standing pages only, and she remembers you pushed.
2. **Ask for protected-escort status** (Leadership or Tactics). A narrower clause covers your escorts, including defending other hulls on Compact lanes.
   - _Success:_ You get explicit protection and priority on contested jobs, in exchange for hours of escort duty and a standing reporting duty to the Compact.
   - _Failure:_ Renata says you want special treatment the Compact cannot afford. The offer stays on the table for one cycle, and berth priority slips.
3. **Take the pages away and let Priya read them** (no roll). You lose nothing today and learn the exact obligations.
   - _Success (automatic):_ Priya reports back with the real terms. The offer stands for one cycle.
   - _Failure:_ None, but Renata notes the delay, and a rival hull may take the berth you wanted.

## 4. Mechanics

- **Core roll:** 2d6 + skill level + characteristic modifier vs a target of 8+ (Traveller-style). Keep it, but hide the maths behind a single "Try it" button. The result shows one of three outcomes: Clean success, Success with a cost (a result of 8+ that triggers a complication, which is where "doing right costs" lives), or Failure that still moves the story. Show the target number only when the player taps "Odds".
- **Characteristics** use the standard six scores of 2-12 (Strength, Dexterity, Endurance, Intelligence, Education, Social Standing). Each gives a modifier of about -2 to +2 (score 10 = +1).
- **Skills** that matter most, range 0-4 (level 0 = basic, 1 = trained, 2+ = expert): Leadership, Pilot, Gunnery, Electronics, Mechanic, Recon, Persuade, Diplomat, Tactics, Gun Combat, Astrogation.
- **Edge:** a pool of 3 per episode. Spending one lets the player re-roll one failed roll or upgrade one Success-with-a-cost into a clean success.
- **Resources tracked:**
  - Credits (the only currency). The alliance must keep a reserve of about Cr40,000 or it cannot cover emergencies.
  - Ship upkeep and fuel.
  - Crew standing (how much each key crew member trusts you).
  - Strain with the Compact (how much Renata's patience you have used).
  - Edge per episode (above).
- **Inventory / ship / crew elements that change choices:** The ship you take on a job (the Pawn is the fast one-armed escort; the Ardent Venture is the large expedition hub with a workshop, sickbay and launch bay) decides which options appear. Crew assigned to a mission add their skills to a roll, and a specialist's offstage report arrives as a short text with one choice. Items such as a sealed data core or probes unlock scenes that a plain inventory would not.

## 5. Visual identity

- **Palette:** deep navy ground, amber for structure and headings, green for status. Both a dark theme and a light theme.
- **UI style:** clean minimal with a faint instrument-panel feel (small caps labels, monospaced numbers). No CRT scanlines.
- **Existing art:** painterly digital portraits for about 40 characters and painterly ship portraits for the four ships, plus a few moody location scenes. A star chart and a hex map exist as SVG files. Art is optional for a first build; the text carries the game.
- **Animation (later):** slow parallax on ship and station views, a subtle shimmer on jump arrivals.

## 6. Later / optional

- **Factions:** the Compact (the station's governing council), Faraday Basin (a yard cooperative that builds and repairs ships), the alliance itself (Marcus's own people), and hijack crews preying on the lanes. More are planned.
- **Key NPCs:** Renata (the Compact's chair), Priya (liaison), Cass (analyst), Miri (young engineer-scientist), Tally (young pilot), Wren (liaison), Lysa (pilot).
- **Central conflict:** whether Marcus can protect the lanes and keep his people lawful and funded while an older mystery about his own past starts to surface.
- **Locations:** Hearthline (home ring-station), the Shoal (a dangerous escort region), Faraday Basin (the yard).
- **Naming conventions:** ships are italicised; the alliance's formal name is "the Lighthouse" and is used sparingly; "Lantern" is a title given to people.
- **Content lines:** mature themes (violence, loss, moral compromise) handled without gore or graphic detail. No sexual content. Teen-and-up.
- **Standalone or adaptation:** an adaptation. The game is a stripped-down version of a long-running solo tabletop campaign, designed so a new player needs no prior knowledge. Use the campaign's names and tone, but a developer should treat the rules as inspiration and avoid reproducing the published tabletop rulebook text or its trademarked setting.

## 7. Added by the owner after this brief

- In the original campaign a large part of the story was that Marcus has **missing memories linking him to people**, and was **running from people pursuing him** or **trying to find the people he remembered from that time**.
- **Ariadne** was the key anchor of that story. No details about her were supplied with this brief; see the open questions in `docs/game-design.md`.
- The game now uses the campaign as source material and a theme guide, not as a literal adaptation.
