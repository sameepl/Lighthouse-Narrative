## Motifs (item 11)

   Recurs: Miri, "Also, did you eat? You look like you didn't eat." (S4E0 l.361).

3. Kept lamps and guests (image)

Readings.
- A typical played scene is 600 to 1,800 words with 1 to 3 player turns. The S4E0 scenes were the longest typical set (about 1,800 words, 3 turns); S4E1 and S4E2 ran many short scenes (about 600 to 750 words, usually 1 turn). S4E2 has 22 scene tags but 21 scene breaks in the log (two tags share one reply).
- A GM reply is typically 275 to 460 words. A player turn is typically 15 to 35 words, so the player writes a sentence or two and the world answers with a page.
- A set piece is the exception: S4E2 has two scenes of 10 exchanges (5,374 and 4,577 words); S3E13 has one of 21 exchanges.
- If one word of reading is taken at an assumed 200 words a minute, a typical scene reads in about 3 to 9 minutes. That is an assumption, not a measurement (TBD).
- Scene count per episode: 6, 12 and 21 (S4E0 to E2); 7, 10 and 11 (S3E11 to E13).

House shape (Core/04_Presentation_and_Voice.md, rules not setting text). Every scene opens with a tag giving place, what Marcus is there to do, and what is at stake. A scene closes on a handed-back moment, never past an open beat. Every scene ends on a visible cost or open consequence. Choice menus are for costly or hard-to-undo decisions; each option states what it gains and costs; the player's typed answer always wins. A scene often closes with a line of the form: Next: [options]. Say "go," or tell me what you do first.

Beat structure. The usual order is setup, complication, choice, cost. Setup is the scene tag plus an NPC putting something in front of the captain. The complication is a fact that makes the obvious move worse. The choice is either typed by the player or offered as two to four options with Gain and Cost. The cost lands at once, as a favour owed, a day lost, a trust spent, or something not learned.

Worked example A (S4E2 l.378 to l.712, one long scene sequence).
- Setup: "Dax, Elias and Ariadne work out how to study the two lamps aboard, and what you must decide before anyone powers either one." (scene tag, l.378)
- Choice with costs: three options, among them "Look only. Cold, by hand, Elias's log, nothing powered." with "Cost: a watch of Elias and Mara." (l.405). Player: "Let's go with 1 first." (l.411)
- Complication: the keepers' channel answers a challenge with the same line each time: "Hold off the marked lamp. The keepers will tend it first." (l.570, l.592).
- Second choice: the menu at l.610 ("what does Marcus do about it?"), option two costing "they will want your word again that no lit lamp is touched." (l.613). Player: "Take it to the real keepers with all the records, and monitor everything they observe." (l.618)
- Cost: the Elder, "I have not heard it since the tea." (l.639), and the log held back; "The convoy has lost a day to the wait" (l.712).

Worked example B (S4E1 l.396 to l.424, one short scene, one exchange).
- Setup: "the stake is whether the alliance is someone they can trust with the rest of the shoal escort job" (scene tag, l.396)
- Complication: a mother asks for a promise that cannot safely be given: "I'll not make you swear." (l.414)
- Choice (typed): "I don't know what I'll find at the end of it. If I can safely tell you first I will." (l.418)
- Cost: "It will be harder for you than it would've been to lie." (l.424)
