# Unnamed roles
Roles are never named. Their register is practical, tired, procedural and courteous: they speak in the paperwork of the place (forms, logs, stamps, slots, seals).
No role knows facts about Ariadne, the pursuers or Marcus's past. If a scene needs one, use [STUB] and ask. Never write Marcus's words. Which slice scene uses each role is (TBD) unless the issue says.
## Berth office clerk or writ clerk
- Wants the form complete and the fee entered before the queue moves. Cites the form and the line, and says "Next."
- Uses whatever name is on the paper for Marcus.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "Name, hull, berth. Arms on line four. Thank you. Next."
## Dock inspector
- Checks seals, counts against the manifest and reads each discrepancy aloud. Polite, unhurried, unmoved.
- Calls him "Captain" or the name on the manifest. Asks about the cargo, never about him.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "Manifest says six. I count six. Sign the seal log and you're clear."
## Stallholder
- Talks prices, weights, and which hauler brought what in. Friendly and quick, and haggles once.
- Customer talk only. Knows nothing about Marcus unless a [STUB] says so.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "Filters are dear this week. The hauler's late. I'll write you a chit."
## Harbourmaster of a hab
- Talks pads, traffic, air and power by the log. Tired and careful, and answers for everyone aboard.
- Addresses the ship by name. States what the hab can and cannot spare.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "Pad two is yours for six hours. The log says that's what we can spare."
## Young keeper of the lamp tender
- Earnest and by the book, proud of the lamp log and the tender's schedule. Age (TBD); if a scene makes them a minor, no on-screen harm and keep them out of danger.
- Calls him "Captain" and reads from the log.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "Lamp's lit on schedule. It's in the log. Do you need it countersigned?"
## Survey crew lead
- Talks grid squares, readings and shift hours. Blunt and practical, and wants the job done and the crew home.
- Treats Marcus as a contractor. Trades readings for help, in writing.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "Grid nine's unmarked. We log it and we leave. That's the contract."
## Convoy or hauler pilot
- Talks slots, lanes and intervals over comms. Terse call and answer, courteous on an open channel.
- Addresses the ship by name ("Errant Pawn"). Never asks about anyone's past.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "Errant Pawn, you're third in the lane. Hold your interval."
## K1's passenger: a transfer clerk (adult, never named)
- Carries a transfer file between offices. Precise, tired, courteous, and worried about stamps and deadlines rather than danger.
- Treats Marcus as the captain carrying them. Never a former crewmate and knows nothing of his old ship. Recognition is shown only by the access-log line on the berth terminal (see the K1 brief), never by the passenger saying anything about Marcus's past.
- [NEW, not for use until approved] "The file must be stamped at the far office by second shift. I'd be grateful, Captain."
