# Trade run that gets interrupted
1. Lost mid-cycle. A life-support-stores run for a habitat board; cold-chain handling eats hold space; a bad lead roll hands the contract to a competitor and the ship carries a partial spot load. Teaches: the market interrupts, no villain. [FROM RECORD: Orc]
2. Bumped slot. The yard does the work well, then moves the ship two days for a larger client; standing is unchanged. Teaches: friction that costs time and trust. [FROM RECORD: Orc]
3. Short of lift. A port authority offers priority mail and records on the mainline, but a passenger block crowds the hold. Offered, not taken; canon only if delivered. [FROM RECORD: Orc]

# Courier of a person or cargo
1. Pay-drop circuit. Sealed envelopes along a five- or six-stop route; the courier takes exactly one drink a shift. The satchel is watched, not taken; a decoy swap fails when he feels a tug. Teaches: tails and swaps cost something even when they fail. [FROM RECORD: S3H]
2. Supplier return. A nervous, honest courier whose appointment was amended irregularly. Options: cancel and seize, an inert duplicate, or interview first. Each loses something. [FROM RECORD: S4P; prepared]
3. Patients to a sanctuary. Off screen, one lead roll in bands: safe admission with a gain, with a cost, a harder obligation, or an urgent supply problem. No off-screen death. [FROM RECORD: S6P]

# Salvage that finds something strange
1. Hauler adrift. Drive cut on a forged order; dead aboard; a survivor found by sound; a bell stuck on the hull by the cut hatch. [FROM RECORD: Plan; layout NEW in Maps]
2. Fast claim. A salvage tender files a lawful claim within about 30 hours of an array going dark; it carries a welded-over patrol turret mount. Teaches: paperwork speed is the clue. [FROM RECORD: Led]
3. Silent buoy. Someone wants a passive relay buoy cleaned before the yard can claim jurisdiction. Evidence comes in proportion to risk, never a full answer. [FROM RECORD: S3P]
